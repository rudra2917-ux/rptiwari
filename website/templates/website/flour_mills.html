A huge market that is still growing

Wheat is a staple across north, central and eastern India. Most families eat roti or chapati every day, and bakeries, biscuit makers and food processors need maida and suji in large volumes.

Where the wheat comes from

Most of India's wheat is grown in the northern and central plains. Mills near these belts save on freight, while mills in the east and south serve large consumer markets.

{% for s in wheat_states %}{{ s }}{% endfor %}

Organised vs. traditional milling

Roller flour mills make most of the country's maida and suji and a growing share of packaged atta. Traditional chakkis, from neighbourhood mills to large industrial chakki plants, still grind most of the wheat eaten as atta. As consumers move to branded and packaged flour, more of this volume is shifting to organised mills.
